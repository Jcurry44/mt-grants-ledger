        The referee

        Halt-on-fail: a year publishes only when it reconciles exactly

        Grant rows come from the Form 990-PF grants-paid schedule — Part XIV in current filings, formerly Part XV. For every tax year, the sum of the grant rows must equal Part I, line 25, column (d) — charitable disbursements — to the dollar. A year that does not tie is withheld, never estimated. All one available years pass; the same checks re-run in your browser’s console on every load.

        This ledger was read from a scan

        Every other ledger in this collection is built from the machine-readable XML the foundation e-filed. This one could not be: M&T’s return reports its entire year of grantmaking as a single line — “See attached statement” — and the 3,105 individual grants live only in an attachment the IRS publishes as a scanned image, with no text layer at all.

        So the rows here were read off that scan by optical character recognition, page by page, and then judged by the same referee as every other year in this collection: the recovered rows must sum to Part I, line 25(d) of the filing. They do, exactly — 3,105 grants totaling $41,009,568, matching both the statement’s own printed total and the filed line, to the dollar. Three recipient names were too faint to read and are shown as unread rather than guessed; their amounts are included, because the amounts are what the total proves.

        Only tax year 2023 appears here. The 2022 scan recovers 99.98% of its total and is $7,640 short — close, but a year that does not reconcile exactly is not published.

        One consequence of a name-and-amount source: the purpose groupings on this page are read entirely from recipients’ filed names — there is no purpose text to read. The rule table below is the complete vocabulary used, and roughly half the dollars stay in “other” rather than being guessed.

          The purpose map

          “Where a granted dollar goes” assigns each grant to one category by the words of the filing itself — first the filed purpose, then (only when the purpose names no field) the recipient’s filed name. Every rule is published below; anything no rule matches is left unassigned rather than guessed. The filed purpose text on every ledger row remains the record.

          Limitations

          Recipient location is the mailing address on the filing, not a service area. Display casing is editorial; hover any name for the as-filed text. Amounts are as filed; no inflation adjustment. Nothing here is an audit, attestation, or opinion — it is a documented reconciliation of the foundation’s own public filings, with the receipts attached.
